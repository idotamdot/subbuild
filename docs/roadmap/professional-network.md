# Placeholder: professional and vendor network

**Status: not implemented.** The current studio can name specialist role categories. It does not list, verify, endorse, price, refer, or place contractors, vendors, engineers, or workers.

## Requested outcome

Provide a trustworthy way to identify appropriately qualified local expertise and compare relevant services for an independently defined project scope.

## Work to plan

- Define which professional specialties, trades, suppliers, and service regions are in scope.
- Set a transparent credential-verification process, authoritative sources, recheck intervals, correction paths, and accessibility requirements.
- Distinguish licensed professional services from vendor listings, advertising, and user-submitted referrals.
- Define conflict disclosures, ranking and payment policies, insurance checks, procurement safeguards, privacy boundaries, and lead-sharing consent.
- Establish what "labor specialty network" means; do not imply worker availability, vetting, employment status, or safety competence without evidence.
- Source all prices, equipment compatibility claims, and vendor suggestions; otherwise label them unknown.

## Acceptance criteria for future implementation

- Every credential and listing has a verifiable source, scope, geography, and last-checked date.
- No vendor or worker is called "approved", "safe", "available", or "vetted" without a documented and current basis.
- Users control what contact details or project information are shared, with whom, and for what purpose.
- The system does not guarantee a contractor, schedule, price, workmanship, or worker-safety outcome.
- Accessibility and anti-discrimination review is completed before public listing or matching.

## Open decisions

Determine operator responsibility, business model, credential categories, liability, independent verification resources, and local professional/legal review before building a directory or matching workflow.
