# Placeholder: jurisdiction and authoritative-source research

**Status: human-led research aid, not a compliance service.** A public county boundary and manually recorded source notes cannot establish parcel jurisdiction, a municipality or ETJ, zoning, licensing, permit requirements, or code compliance.

## Requested outcome

Help a user identify appropriate authorities and primary sources for a future project discussion, record what was checked and when, and make uncertainty or conflicting requirements conspicuous.

## Work to plan

- Separate public county boundary references from parcel, municipal, ETJ, and permitting determinations.
- Provide official agency sources; store source identity, scope, effective date/edition, retrieval/check date, and the exact claim a reviewer checked.
- Keep search results as leads until a person checks the original source and records their review.
- Provide an escalation path for conflicting, out-of-date, missing, or out-of-scope sources.
- Establish update/correction procedures and identify the qualified role responsible for each source class.
- Threat-model integrations; do not send an address, parcel identifier, or precise coordinates without separate consent and privacy review.

## Acceptance criteria for future implementation

- Every requirement statement links to an authoritative source and an identified human review state.
- County geography is never used to infer parcel-level jurisdiction or code approval.
- Expired, conflicting, or missing source data is labeled unresolved and cannot be displayed as verified.
- No automated output is labeled legal advice, code interpretation, permit approval, license verification, or safety certification.
- Decisions requiring site facts are deferred to the relevant authority and appropriately licensed professional.

## Open decisions

Define geographic scope, authoritative-source curation and maintenance, qualified reviewers, privacy constraints for site data, and escalation ownership before extending research automation.
